# Vinegar Production Notes

## Overview

Vinegar production is a two-stage fermentation: yeast first converts sugar to ethanol, and acetic acid bacteria then oxidise the ethanol to acetic acid. The second stage is aerobic, which is what separates it from the anaerobic lactic fermentation used for preserving vegetables.

## Acetic Acid Pathway

Acetic acid bacteria sit at the surface of the liquid where oxygen is available, and they convert ethanol to acetic acid with acetaldehyde as an intermediate. The reaction releases heat and consumes oxygen, so a culture that is stirred too gently stalls for lack of oxygen rather than for lack of substrate.

## Oxygen Requirement

The vessel is deliberately kept partly empty so that the surface film has air, and the liquid is never filled to the neck. Sealing a vinegar culture airtight stops the conversion entirely, unlike a lactic fermentation where the seal is required.
